Research

My work spans the evaluation, adaptation, and efficient deployment of large language models and AI agents.

Research Interests

Agent Evaluation

I work on evaluating models and agents through benchmark suites, automated checks, sandboxed execution, and LLM judges. At Stanford GSB’s AIM-Lab, I studied judge reliability and biases including position, verbosity, and self-preference.

Stanford AIM-Lab experience →

Efficient Inference & Model Routing

At GMI Cloud, I work on cost-aware routing that predicts per-query quality and inference cost across models. This includes model evaluation at scale, step-level model switching for agent trajectories, and quantized embedding inference.

GMI Cloud experience →

Inference Quality Monitoring & Reliable Delivery

I study how inference-time choices change what a model actually does, and how to gate deployments on that evidence. Holding the checkpoint, prompts, sampling, and scoring fixed while varying only the serving stack isolates the effect of quantization, speculative decoding, and the tool-call and structured-output layer on task accuracy, so that a serving change is accepted on measured accuracy rather than on latency alone. A recurring finding is that tool-calling failures often originate in the serving stack rather than in the model weights.

Human–AI Interaction

Static benchmarks score the artifact, not the collaboration. Through trustingdata.ai at Stanford GSB’s AIM-Lab I build human-in-the-loop evaluations of LLMs and agents, and I’m interested in how far simulated users can stand in for real ones before the two diverge.

Self-Improving AI Agents

How orchestrators and subagents should adapt from execution feedback: when to keep searching, revise prompts, or fine-tune jointly, and how to validate each change before adopting it. Current work on orchestrator–subagent coevolution is under review.
